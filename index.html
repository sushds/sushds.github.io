accountability.

I'm working on problems where data models have real stakes — sustainability disclosures that end up in annual reports, ML systems that run at scale, and governance frameworks built from scratch without a clear template.

At bol, I own the CO₂ emissions data models that feed board-level sustainability disclosures, validated at reasonable assurance every quarter. Before that, risk and controls consulting at Protiviti, modern data stack builds at Apolix, and an MSc in Business Analytics from TIAS School for Business and Society, Tilburg University, Cum Laude.

I'm also drawn to questions that don't have clean answers yet — how AI compute should be governed, how AI will affect our society, whether ESG disclosures are actually verifiable, what good decision-making looks like under structural uncertainty. That curiosity tends to show up in everything I build.

Research & thinking

In progress — research paper
Are Scope 1/2 emissions disclosures in Dutch ESG equity funds actually verifiable?
Cross-validating company-reported emissions from Dutch ESG fund holdings against EU ETS verified data from the EUTL. The question is whether ESRS E1 disclosures, as they currently stand, are comparable, internally consistent, and independently auditable at portfolio level. Open-source pipeline using CDP, EUTL, and public fund holdings data. Targeted at SSRN and PyData Amsterdam.
In progress — paper
AI compute hardware and governance — who controls the infrastructure of intelligence?
Exploring how concentrations in AI compute — chip manufacturing, data centre ownership, cloud provision — create governance gaps that regulation hasn't caught up with. Background in internal controls and high-stakes data systems makes this less abstract than it sounds.
